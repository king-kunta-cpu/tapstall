Ten modules

One operating day

The console is not a dashboard bolted onto a spreadsheet. Each module is a step of the same loop, and each writes to the same ledger.

01

Overview & ECU

The operating day at a glance, with the exception control unit naming what is overdue, stuck, or waiting on a person.

02

Units

Every unit of work — a candidate, a campaign, a stock line — with its owner, its state and its history.

03

wCells

Work cells: the small, bounded queues that a thing moves through. A cell has an entry rule and an exit rule, not a mood.

04

Lead hunting

Country-specific sources swept on a schedule, deduplicated and admitted under rules rather than scraped into a list.

05

Admission

Nothing enters the pipeline without passing the gate. A record that fails is refused before it exists, not deleted after.

06

Scoring

Each candidate is scored against a real profile, and the score kept with the evidence that produced it.

07

Outreach

Drafts prepared for a human decision. Approval sends from the operator's own mailbox; rejection removes the artefacts on the spot.

08

Approvals

The queue where a person spends a minute and the machine spends a day. Every decision is attributed.

09

Touch log

Who was contacted, when, from which mailbox, and what came back — including the do-not-contact list, which nothing overrides.

10

Ledger

The append-only record that makes the other nine auditable. If it is not in the ledger, it did not happen.

How a unit moves

Same loop, every time
  1. Discovery

    Sources are swept inside a quota. A sweep that would exceed it waits; it does not silently truncate.

  2. Admission

    The gate applies the rules — region, duplicates, the do-not-contact ledger — and refuses before a record is created.

  3. Preparation

    Work is drafted against facts the operator can check. No invented history, no invented numbers.

  4. Decision

    A person approves or rejects. Nothing with an outward effect happens without that tap.

  5. Effect

    Approval sends and is attributed; rejection deletes the drafts. Either way the ledger keeps the decision, not just the outcome.

  6. Review

    The exception control unit surfaces what failed and why — the part most tools leave to memory.

What it refuses to do

Design, not policy
It never sends unattended

A person approves each outward action. The machine can prepare a day's work; it cannot commit it.

It never invents a record

Every field traces to a source the operator can inspect. Where the evidence is thin, the output says so.

It never overrides the do-not-contact list

The exclusion is checked at admission, so a blocked party is never prepared for in the first place.

It never hides a failure

Runs are bounded and fail loudly, with the log streaming. A quiet failure is treated as a defect.

The case study

DeskOS is examined as a bounded design-science case rather than a product-success claim: what was built, what it refused to do, and which of its failures were structural. The method is in the book; the console stays with the operator.
