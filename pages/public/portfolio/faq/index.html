What is autonomous cognitive infrastructure?

It is the layer above an application: the part that decides what to do next, records why, and can be audited afterwards. An app waits for a person to press a button; this layer hunts, drafts, checks, files and escalates — and writes down every step so a human can disagree with it later. Courage Garikai builds it for markets where bandwidth, power and trust are all constrained, and writes about it in the working papers in the research section.

Are the books free?

Where a work is marked CC BY 4.0 it is free to read, download and reuse with attribution. Where it is marked all rights reserved it is free to read here but not to republish. Two works are catalogued without a public edition, and their pages say so rather than offering a link that goes nowhere. Permissions lists every work and its licence in one table.

What formats do the works come in?

PDF, EPUB, HTML and Markdown for every text-native work — one Markdown source, typeset four ways, so a reader needs no particular app and can read offline. The HTML edition is also served as a reading page here, so a work can be read in the browser without downloading anything.

Are the research papers peer reviewed?

No, and no page says they are. They are working papers and a field report in the DCAI series: bounded, single-case, and explicit about what has not been proven, who the sample was, and what would falsify the claim. A working paper is not dressed as a journal article here.

Can I quote, teach from, or republish a work?

Quote briefly with attribution and a link — always fine. Teaching: CC BY 4.0 works may be used in a course with attribution. Republishing a whole work, translating it, or printing it for sale: write first, whether the licence strictly requires it or not. Permissions has the details and a citation format.

Do you hold merchant money?

No. The commerce rail is built so that money moves directly between a buyer and the merchant's own account; the system records and reconciles, it does not hold. That is a design rule, not a policy that could quietly change — the platform's tests fail if custody is introduced.

Do you use AI to build the systems, or write the books?

Both answers are yes and neither is a secret. The systems are agentic by design — that is the subject. The production of the books and this site is partly scripted: the catalogue, the jacket artwork, the share cards and these pages are generated from one repository, and the pages say which. The arguments, the cases and the failures come from systems that were run, and the parts that were constructed rather than observed are labelled as such in the work itself. A reader is entitled to know which is which, and the works are written to make that possible.

How is this site built?

One repository, one Python script per artefact, no framework and no database. How it is built walks through it, including what it costs to run and what breaks the build on purpose.

How does a consulting engagement start?

With a message that says what the system has to do, who uses it, and what happens today when it fails. That is enough for a first conversation. If it is not a fit, that is said in the first call rather than in a proposal nobody believes. There is no scarcity clock and no availability theatre on this site — the consulting section says why.

Is the site accessible?

It is built to be: semantic headings, keyboard paths, visible focus, no autoplay that cannot be stopped, and text contrast measured rather than eyeballed. The accessibility statement states what was tested, what the numbers are, and what is known to fall short.
