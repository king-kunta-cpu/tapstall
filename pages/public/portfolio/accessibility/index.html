Accessibility

Built to be read: no client-side rendering, no third-party assets, measured contrast, and an honest list of what falls short.

The approach

The site is hand-built HTML with no framework, no client-side rendering and no third-party assets. That removes a whole class of accessibility failures at the root: there is no injected widget to fight, no font that fails to load, no script that decides whether the text appears. What remains is typography, structure and focus — the parts that can be checked.

What was actually tested

  • Keyboard-only navigation through every page: tab order follows the page, the skip targets are real headings, focus is visible.
  • Text zoom to 200 % and 400 % at a 1280 px and a 390 px viewport, checking that nothing is clipped and the page does not scroll sideways.
  • Rendering at 390, 768, 1024, 1280 and 1440 px; scrollWidth is asserted against the viewport width on the catalogue, every work page, the reading editions and the DeskOS page, because a page that scrolls sideways is a page that cannot be read on a phone.
  • Contrast, calculated rather than eyeballed. Body text on paper is 16.8:1; the secondary grey is 4.7:1; the accent red used for links and kickers is 6.2:1; the light grey on the dark panel in the editions is 10.7:1. All exceed the 4.5:1 required for normal text (the accent is used above 18.66 px bold or for non-text accents where it does not carry meaning on its own).
  • Reduced motion: if your system asks for less movement, the two looping images — the avatar in the consulting section and the floating contact button — are replaced by their still frames, and the hover transforms are switched off.

Structure

One h1 per page, headings in order after it, landmarks (header, main, nav, footer), a skip path through the masthead, and links whose text says where they go — no "click here". Decorative images carry empty alt; jackets carry the title they belong to.

Animation

Two things on this site move: the avatar beside the consulting heading, and the floating contact button. Both are GIFs, so they cannot be paused by a control — that is a real limitation and it is stated rather than hidden. Both respect prefers-reduced-motion, both are small, and neither carries information that is lost if it stops. Nothing else on the site animates on its own.

Where it falls short

  • The PDFs are not reflowable. A fixed 6 × 9 page does not re-hex at 400 % zoom. This is why every text-native work also ships as HTML and EPUB, and why the HTML reading edition is one click from each work page.
  • The reading editions are the book's own design. Wide tables inside them scroll inside their own box on a small screen rather than shrinking the type.
  • No formal audit. The checks above are the author's own. No third-party accessibility audit has been commissioned, and the site does not claim conformance it has not had tested. If that changes, this page will say so.
  • One author. There is no accessibility team and no ticketing system; there is an email address, and it is read.

If something blocks you

Write to couragegarikai@atomicmail.io and say what you were trying to do and what happened instead. Barrier reports are treated as defects, not as feature requests, and fixed in the next build of the site. If you need any work in another form — a large-print PDF, a plain-text file, a differently structured document — ask, and it will be produced.
