# The distribution problem — how 180 shops and 12 agencies actually happen

The code is not the constraint. The constraint is that we have a product for people who are busy
running a counter, and no list of them. This file is the plan for that, in the order that costs
least and proves most.

## The one-line answer

**Stop selling to shops. Sell to the people who already own the shops, and make the first ten of
them co-owners of the pitch.**

An agency that already manages 30 merchants is worth 30 cold calls we never make — and unlike a
shop, an agency is *paid to evaluate software*. Their whole job is finding tools that make their
clients' lives easier.

## Who to target (in order)

1. **Website and WhatsApp-marketing agencies** in Harare, Bulawayo, Johannesburg, Cape Town,
   Nairobi. They have merchants, they take a retainer, and they cannot currently give a client a
   working order rail.
2. **POS resellers and hardware installers** — they sell the terminal, they have no software.
   A rail is the natural upsell and it does not compete with their hardware.
3. **Bookkeepers and accountants** — they do month-end for 20–40 shops, and the settlement
   statement is the artefact that saves them a day. They do not want to sell it; they want to
   *recommend* it. That is better.
4. **Market associations and chambers** — Mbare traders' groups, salon associations, restaurant
   groups, church-run business networks. One demo to a room of forty is forty leads in an hour.
5. **Individual shops** — only after 1–3, and only inbound. Door-knocking is the most expensive
   hour we own.

## The offer, in three tiers (so the second sentence is never "how much?")

| Tier | Who | Price | What they get |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Pilot** | First 10 agencies | $0 for 60 days | Full white label, up to 10 merchants, our phone number, and a written case study they approve |
| **Partner** | Standard | **$149/mo** | Their brand, 5 seats included, $29 per extra merchant, settlement statements, priority support |
| **Platform** | Resellers, 100+ | **$299/mo** | Unlimited seats, sub-agency accounts, their own pricing, co-branded onboarding |

Rule that keeps this honest and useful: **the pilot is free but not open-ended.** It ends on a
date, with a decision meeting already booked on day one.

## The first 30 days (a calendar, not a mood)

| Week | Action | Target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Build the list: 60 agencies + 30 accountants, with a name, a number and one specific hook each ("your client list sells catering — your site has no order button") | 90 names, no duplicates |
| 1 | Publish the *settlement statement* sample on the site. It is the only asset that makes an agency lean in | live |
| 2 | 20 WhatsApp openers a day, 4 days. Not a pitch — one question and one screenshot | 80 openers, 15 replies |
| 2 | 10-minute demo, one per day. Screen-share the statement, never the roadmap | 5 demos |
| 3 | Convert 3 pilots. Set the day-60 decision in the calendar before they say yes | 3 pilots, 3 dates |
| 3 | Ask each pilot for one introduction to another agency. This is the whole growth engine | 3 intros |
| 4 | First paid Partner. Take the $149 even if the pilot is running — paying changes behaviour | 1 paying |
| 4 | Write the case study from the first pilot's own words, with their numbers | 1 page |

**Month 2–3:** two more Partners, one Platform reseller, and the first invoice paid *because the
screenshot of the statement convinced them*, not because we asked.

## The opener (WhatsApp, the market's own channel)

> Hi {name} — do you manage websites for shops around {area}? I built an order rail that gives a
> shop a proper storefront link and matches its EcoCash/InnBucks payments to the right order
> automatically. Instead of selling it shop by shop I'm giving it to ten agencies under their own
> brand for 60 days, free. Want me to send you the one-page statement a merchant gets at 07:00?
> If it's not useful, delete it — no call needed.

Why this works: it names their business, offers something they can *show a client tomorrow*,
carries a real deadline, and removes the meeting as a precondition. No "quick question", no
"circling back", no invented urgency.

## What we measure (and the numbers that end the experiment)

| Metric | Target by day 60 | If missed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opener → reply | 15% | the list is wrong, not the offer — rebuild the list before rewriting copy |
| Demo → pilot | 50% | the demo shows features instead of the statement; show only the statement |
| Pilot → paid | 40% | we are a free tool that people like and do not need — raise the pain or drop the tier |
| Merchants per agency | 8+ | the agency is reselling nothing; teach them the onboarding, not the software |
| Referrals per pilot | 1 | the pilot did not actually use it |

**Kill rule, written now:** if 3 pilots produce no invoices by day 60, stop selling agencies and go
back to one segment — accountants — with one artefact. Do not add features to fix distribution.

## Why the product is already on our side here

- The **settlement statement** is a screen an agency can put in front of a client and say "this is
  your month". Nobody else in these markets gives them that.
- The **white label** means the agency's brand gets the credit. They will sell a thing that makes
  *them* look good far harder than a thing that makes us look good.
- The **free tier** means no procurement conversation: a merchant can start today, which is the
  only kind of start that exists in a cash business.
- The **emotion doctrine** is the reason the pitch above works: relief, ownership, and the pride
  of being the first shop on the street to answer properly. Nothing invented.

## What I would refuse to do

- Buy an email list. It burns the domain and the market is small enough to remember us.
- Run a discount countdown. It contradicts the honesty clause and cheapens the statement.
- Sell a "reseller programme" with revenue promises we cannot underwrite.
- Take a shop that an agency brought us and bill them directly. That is how an agency network
  dies in one conversation.
