The Ledger and the Shopkeeper

Building commerce for the markets the platforms forgot

A trade book on building a merchant rail for shops the platforms skipped — written from the release log rather than the pitch deck. Part one is the gap: the forty-tab panic, and why the obvious answer is wrong. Part two is the machine: a shop in one link, a statement that can be recomputed, quotas as a design material, the first hundred prospects, and the number that could not be dialled. Part three is the rules: never hold the money, never lie in the copy, never post to your customers. Part four is the craft — what broke, in five failure classes, and the uncomfortable arithmetic that follows.

Intelligence, Data and Judgement

An applied introduction to artificial intelligence, with a production case

A textbook written to be taught from. Twenty-two chapters in five parts — foundations, the material, the machine, the case, and judgement — each with learning outcomes, a worked example, graded exercises and further reading. The disciplinary spine is three widely used curricula: COMCEC's introduction to AI and its fundamental concepts, Microsoft's Fundamentals of AI Module 1, and NITI Aayog's Let's Learn AI Base Module, each attributed and mapped chapter by chapter in the appendix. Tapstall runs through the middle of the book as the worked case, including its failures.

Eleven tables, two pseudocode listings, a sixty-term glossary, eight labs and an annotated bibliography.

Order Without Custody

Governance-first design for merchant commerce rails in low-infrastructure economies

Small merchants already sell at scale — through chat threads, pages and a phone number painted on a wall — but they sell without a record. This paper argues that the binding constraint at that tier is not payment capability but reconciliation, and that reconcilability is an architectural property rather than a feature. Five mechanisms are formalised against artefacts a reader can inspect: the No-Custody Rail, Admission-Gated Discovery, Reserve-Ladder Scheduling, Surface-Disjoint Multi-Tenancy and the Constrained Persuasion Doctrine. The empirical section is forensic: five classes of governance failure, each stated as a signature, a detector and a design response.

Candid about its limits: no paying customers in the measurement window, no control condition, no inference beyond the case.

About

Jacaranda Labs Press · Harare

I write about commerce in places where the assumptions baked into software — always-on power, card rails, a postcode, a credit history — are simply false. Then I build the thing the writing describes.

That practice is Tapstall: a merchant rail for Zimbabwe, South Africa, Kenya and Nigeria that gives a shop a storefront, an order link, a one-tap buyer confirmation and a settlement statement that reconciles orders against money the merchant received directly. It holds no customer funds, charges a flat fee, and runs on free infrastructure. The engineering is documented in public — releases, a test suite, a failure log — and this catalogue is what comes out of it.

The books are published under a Creative Commons licence where I hold the rights, and produced as Markdown first. The page you are reading was generated by a script from the same repository the books live in, so the catalogue cannot drift from the files.
